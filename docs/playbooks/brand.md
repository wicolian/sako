# Brand playbook: voice, positioning and visual rules

How we defined the brand for a productized creative service: what we stood for, how we sounded, how things looked, and how we planned the brand to change as the company grew.

## How to use this

- Use it as a template for a short brand document that a small team can follow without a design agency.
- Keep the structure: essence, enemy, voice with examples, visual rules, content rules, and a plan for change.
- Replace our colors, fonts and jokes with yours. The method is the useful part.

## 1. Essence and positioning

**Essence:** predictable creative throughput. Design, motion and video with delivery clocks and proof.

**Positioning:** we are not an agency and not a freelancer network. We are the reliability layer of creative work: measurable, scalable and calm.

**Promise:** no meetings, no maybes, just delivery.

**Brand enemy:** chaos. That means meetings, missed deadlines and rework loops.

**Core principle:** creativity deserves uptime.

### Where the personality came from

| Borrowed from | Trait |
|---|---|
| A calm car brand | Precision, clarity, confidence |
| A bold analytics company | Dry humor, intelligence, self-awareness |
| A database platform | Blunt, efficient, minimal |
| Enterprise vendors | Metrics-first reliability |

Pick three or four public brands you admire. Name the one trait you take from each. It stops a team from arguing about taste.

### The sales version of the positioning

> "We are a design pod for B2B SaaS growth teams."

The brand line was big and poetic. The sales line was plain. Use both. Use the plain one when you talk to a buyer. See `sales-playbook.md`.

## 2. Brand architecture at a glance

| Layer | Version 1 choice |
|---|---|
| Essence | Predictable creative throughput |
| Tone | Bold, dry, funny, weird (calm later) |
| Colors | Purple and lime, neutral greys |
| Type | Three fonts from one family (sans, serif, mono) |
| Voice | Witty, short, confident, proof-based |
| Visuals | Brutalist calm, moving toward minimal elegance |
| Goal of version 1 | Get traction. Stand out. Be remembered. |
| Goal of version 2 | Earn respect. Look inevitable. |

## 3. Version 1: bold, dry, weird

**When to use it:** at the start, before you have traction. The reasoning: "You do not earn attention with calm. You earn calm by surviving the noise."

### Voice rules

| Trait | What it means | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Dry confidence | Say less. Mean more. | "We do not brainstorm. We deliver." |
| Controlled humor | One dry punchline at most | "This meeting could have been a metric." |
| Meta honesty | Talk like you know the game | "Yes, it is another SaaS. But this one actually ships decks." |
| Brutal clarity | No marketing adjectives | "48h SLAs. Proof dashboards. Done." |

### Taglines we used or considered

- "Creative Ops. Productized."
- "We get sh*t done."
- "Predictable Pixels."
- "Not an agency. Infrastructure."

### Example lines

- "We deliver pixels like uptime."
- "Creative chaos is cute until it costs you a client."
- "This was delivered under SLA."
- "Our chaos is managed. Yours can be too."

### Colors (version 1)

| | Light mode | Dark mode |
|---|---|---|
| Background | rgb(250, 250, 250) | rgb(27, 27, 27) |
| Foreground | rgb(26, 26, 26) | rgb(250, 250, 250) |
| Primary | rgb(70, 58, 177), purple | rgb(154, 232, 62), lime |
| Text on primary | rgb(250, 250, 250) | rgb(26, 26, 26) |

### Typography

- Sans (body, interface, posts): a geometric sans.
- Serif (headlines, manifesto): a display serif from the same family.
- Mono (metrics and dashboards): a mono from the same family.

We used one open font family with all three styles. One family keeps the system coherent and cheap. Feel: geometric, rational, slightly brutalist.

### Visual system

- Bold blocks of text.
- A monochrome palette with one accent.
- Plenty of white space.
- A still page with loud words.
- A 12-column grid.
- Imagery that shows operations, not decoration.
- Motion: fast cuts, micro-pauses, snappy transitions.

### Content behavior

Start on one platform. We started on LinkedIn.

| Format | Purpose | Tone |
|---|---|---|
| Founder posts | Thought and wit | 70% bold, 30% calm |
| Company page | Proof and precision | 60% calm, 40% bold |
| Videos | Chaos to calm story | Hero film and memes |
| Carousels | Education and metrics | Deadpan wit |

### Launch assets

- [ ] **Hero film.** Story arc: chaos, freeze, calm, proof. Cut it at 60, 15 and 6 seconds.
- [ ] **Proof dashboard shots.** Weekly posts.
- [ ] **A bold landing page.** Text-heavy. One strong call-to-action button.
- [ ] **Dry taglines in the product interface.**

### Version 1 goals and KPIs

Goals we set: three working delivery pods, a first meaningful revenue milestone, and a community that believes in async work. (Planning goals. We did not reach them all.)

KPIs we set: awareness over aesthetics, 20 or more inbound leads a month, one or more paying clients from social in 30 days, and a 50% or better hero film watch rate. Targets, not results.

## 4. Version 2: calm, elegant, polished

**When to use it:** after traction. "When the system works, silence becomes power."

| Trait | Description | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Calm confidence | Authority through stillness | "Predictability is the new creativity." |
| Philosophical minimalism | Let white space speak | "While others chase inspiration, we build infrastructure." |
| Proof and precision | Data as story | "Every SLA. Every task. Public." |
| Poetic control | Simple rhythm | "We measure chaos so you never feel it." |

Visual language for version 2:

- Layout: generous white space, centered grids.
- Type: serif-led. Serif for hero lines, sans for proof lines.
- Color: lime becomes the main color. Purple becomes secondary.
- Motion: cinematic, long pauses, breathing visuals.
- Photography: filmic, material, tactile, neutral daylight.

Signature film: "This is us." Calm and philosophical. Structure: observation, obsession, precision, defiance, future. 90 to 120 seconds, low calm voice-over. The aim is to move from product to belief.

Content system for version 2:

| Asset | Tone | Objective |
|---|---|---|
| Manifesto essay | Elegant, reflective | Define the category |
| Investor deck | Clean, confident | Raise money and set the image |
| Product case films | Proof and beauty | Enterprise trust |
| Website v2 | Minimal, cinematic | Public credibility |

Taglines: "Predictability is the new creativity." and "Reliability is not optional. It is our product."

## 5. How to move from version 1 to version 2

Plan the switch by trigger, not by date.

| Trigger | Signal | Action |
|---|---|---|
| Three working delivery teams | Operational scale | Start rolling out the calm palette |
| A revenue milestone | Proof of traction | Add serif type and the version 2 voice |
| A funding event | Validation | Launch the film and manifesto site |

Bridge rules:

- Keep the founders bold. Make the company voice composed.
- Replace jokes with proof visuals.
- Move from memes to mini-documentaries.
- Shift the hero color from purple to lime in small steps.

We planned four stages: a bold launch era, a bridge, a calm era, then a credible-leader era. In practice we closed before the later stages. Do not spend design time on version 2 before you have customers.

**Guiding principle:** stage 1, be loud when no one is listening. Stage 2, be silent when everyone is watching. Stage 3, be inevitable.

## 6. Writing rules for sales and support (from the sales playbook)

- Say "design pod for SaaS growth teams". Do not say "design subscription service". Nobody knows what it means.
- Say "unlimited revisions until it matches the brief". Do not say "unlimited requests". It sounds too good to be true.
- Do not say "sorry to bother you" or "is this a good time".
- Agree with the buyer about AI where it is true. Point to the gap where it is not.
- Show proof. Offer a real 48-hour trial asset before a PDF.

## 7. Checklist: build your own one-page brand

- [ ] One sentence of essence
- [ ] A promise a buyer can check
- [ ] A named enemy
- [ ] Three or four traits, each with a real example line
- [ ] One accent color, one neutral, light and dark values
- [ ] One font family
- [ ] Rules for each channel and format
- [ ] A small set of launch assets (film, proof screens, landing page)
- [ ] Triggers for when the brand should grow up

## Adapt it

- The bold, weird tone worked for a company that needed to be noticed. If your buyers are conservative, start calm.
- Your tone should match how your buyer talks, not how you talk.
- Brand did not solve our hardest problem, which was reaching buyers. Do not let a nice brand stand in for a sales process.
