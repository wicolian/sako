# Sales playbook: outbound calls, demos and objections

How we ran outbound sales for a design and motion subscription aimed at B2B SaaS growth teams. It covers who to call, how to prepare, what to say, how to follow up, and when to change course.

## How to use this

- Use it if you sell a productized service to a narrow buyer by phone, video call and email.
- Do not copy the scripts word for word. Keep the shape: observation, pain question, small ask.
- Replace the product facts (price, 48-hour promise, guarantee) with your own.
- The numbers marked "target" were goals we set. They are not proven results.

## 1. Core positioning (use on every call)

> "We are a design pod for B2B SaaS growth teams. We clear the design backlog so growth teams ship faster, without hiring, freelancer chaos, or agency lock-in."

Memorize it. Everything else is supporting detail.

## 2. The offer in one table

| Plan | Price | Active slots | Team seats | Best for |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Core | $2,500 a month | 1 | 2 | One steady stream of work |
| Ops Base | $4,500 a month | 2 | 4 | Two streams in parallel |
| Enterprise | From $8,500 a month | 3 or more | 5 or more | Many teams, governance |

Key points to say out loud:

- Delivery in 48 business hours, backed by an SLA with service credits if we miss it.
- Month-to-month billing. No contract.
- 14-day money-back guarantee on all plans.
- Motion is in the same slot as design. It is not a second vendor.
- Pause and resume. Unused days are banked.
- Unlimited brands in one workspace.
- Unlimited revisions "until it matches the brief". (Never say "unlimited requests". See the phrases to avoid.)

See `pricing-and-unit-economics.md` and `product-and-service.md` for the full detail.

## 3. Who to call (ICP)

| Fit | Profile | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Strong | B2B SaaS, seed to Series B, 20 to 150 people, active marketing or growth team, ships campaigns every week | The design backlog is a real, felt problem |
| Decent | B2B SaaS, bootstrapped or profitable, 10 to 50 people, one designer stretched thin, uses freelancers now and then | Cost-aware. Values efficiency. |
| Bad | Not SaaS, no product yet, needs enterprise procurement, needs 3D or VFX, or is hunting for the cheapest bid | Do not call. Remove from the list. |

### Titles to call

| Budget holders (call these) | Influencers (can refer you up) |
|---|---|
| Head of Marketing or CMO | Design lead or art director |
| Head of Growth | Content marketing manager |
| VP Marketing | Growth marketing manager |
| Founder or CEO (companies under 30 people) | Product marketing manager |
| Marketing manager (companies under 30 people) | Demand gen manager |

### Market tiers

| Tier | Where | Priority | Why |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | US, UK, Canada, Germany, France, Netherlands, Nordics, Australia | Primary | The price is normal SaaS tool spend here |
| 2 | UAE, Singapore, Israel, Brazil, South Korea, Indian companies that sell to the US | Secondary | Growing ecosystems, lower cost to win, more price sensitive |
| 3 | The rest of Latin America, Africa, most of South-East Asia, Eastern Europe | Do not chase | Take inbound only |

Tag leads in your CRM with standard region codes (for example NAM, EMEA, DACH, ANZ, APAC, NORDICS). It keeps reports clean.

### Red flags (not a fit)

- Fewer than about three design tasks a month. A freelancer is cheaper for them.
- They need same-day turnaround every time.
- Print, packaging or physical product design.
- Full website code. We did design and builds from approved designs only.
- Very specialized work (medical illustration, 3D rendering).
- They will not subscribe until they win their own deals.

### Qualification questions

1. How do you handle design today? Freelancers, agency or in-house?
2. About how many design requests do you have each month?
3. Do you need motion or video, or only static design?
4. What turnaround do you expect?
5. Do you have brand guidelines?
6. What do you spend on creative work each month?
7. Have you tried a design subscription before? What worked and what did not?

## 4. Pre-call research: three minutes, no more

The goal is one good opening line. It is not a dossier.

**Minute 1: the website.** Find one real observation about design, UX or visual hierarchy. Scan in this order: hero, value proposition, call to action, visual hierarchy, pricing page, product screenshots, mobile.

Observation templates:

- "Your value proposition sits below the fold and the demo button competes with three other links."
- "Your platform page is deep, but the visual hierarchy makes it hard to scan."
- "Your pricing section feels cut off from the story above it."
- "Your product screenshots could sell for you, but they are small and hard to read."
- "Your hero says [vague tagline]. I had to scroll three sections to see what you do."
- "Your blog and landing pages look like different people made them."
- "There is no social proof above the fold."

**Minute 2: the stage.** A quick look at their funding stage tells you how to pitch.

| Stage | How it shapes the call |
|---|---|
| Seed or pre-seed | Small team. The founder decides. Lead with cost and founder-to-founder talk. |
| Series A | Growing fast. Needs weekly campaign output. Best fit. |
| Series B and later | Many teams fight for design time. Pitch parallel slots (Ops Base). |
| Bootstrapped | Lead with the cost math against a full-time hire. |
| Just raised | Money to spend, urgency is high. Great timing. |

**Minute 3: the person.** Change your framing by role.

| Role | Frame the call around |
|---|---|
| Founder or CEO | Speed. "You should not be art directing freelancers." |
| Head of Marketing | Throughput. "Your team should not wait on design to ship campaigns." |
| Head of Growth | Experiment speed. "You cannot test four landing page variants if each takes two weeks." |
| Marketing manager | Looking good inside the company. "Focus on strategy. We handle production." |

### Pre-call notepad (fill in on paper)

- Prospect and date
- Observation
- Stage, and whether they raised recently
- Person and title
- The one question you will ask

## 5. The call script

Target length: 3 to 5 minutes. The goal is to learn if design throughput is a real pain and to book a 15-minute walkthrough. Do not talk price. Do not try to close on this call.

### Phase 1: opener (first 20 seconds)

> "Hey [name], this is [your name]. I run a design studio. I was looking at [company]'s site and had a quick thought about [specific observation]. Got 2 minutes?"

Do not open with "How are you", "Is this a good time", or "We are a design subscription". The observation proves you did the work.

| They say | You say |
|---|---|
| "Sure, go ahead" | Repeat the observation. Move to discovery. No thank-you and no "awesome". |
| "I am busy" | "Totally fair. Tomorrow morning or end of week, which is better?" Give two options only. |
| "Not interested" | "No worries. Quick question before I go: do you handle design in-house or with freelancers?" You get information either way. |
| "Who is this?" | Say your name again, calmly, then the observation. |

### Phase 2: discovery (2 to 4 minutes)

> "I talk to a lot of B2B SaaS teams at your stage. The pattern I keep seeing is that the design queue becomes a bottleneck once you ship marketing assets next to product. Landing pages pile up, ads get stuck, decks get rushed. Is that something you deal with?"

Then stop talking. Let them fill the silence.

| They say | You say |
|---|---|
| "Yeah, actually..." | "What has been stuck the longest? How long has it sat? Who handles design now?" Dig. Do not pitch. |
| "We have a designer" | "Do they cover product only, or GTM too? The in-house person usually gets pulled in five directions and marketing work slips." |
| "We use an agency" | "How is that going? Most teams say it works until project three or four, then turnaround slips." If they defend the agency hard, they are not your buyer right now. |
| "We use freelancers" | "Reliable ones? That is usually the first thing to break when volume grows." Keep it short. |
| "We just use AI tools" | "Makes sense for social and templates. It breaks on anything that has to convert: landing pages, ad creative, decks. Those need judgment, not generation." |
| "No budget" | "Is it a freeze across the board, or is design not a line item right now?" A freeze means check back in 60 days. No line item means wrong person. |
| "Send me an email" | "Will do. Quick question: who owns design decisions on your team?" |
| "Not the right person" | "Who would be the right person for marketing assets and design?" Get the name. Call that person next. |

### Phase 3: the close (30 seconds)

Use it only when the pain is confirmed. You are closing for a 15-minute walkthrough, not for the subscription.

> "I will be straight with you. We are new. I am the founder and I am personally on every project right now. I would like to take one asset you need, a landing page, a deck, an ad set, whatever has been stuck, and turn it around in 48 hours so you can see the quality. Month to month, no contract, 14-day money-back. Is it worth 15 minutes to walk through how it works?"

| They say | You say |
|---|---|
| "Yes" | Offer two time slots. Book on the call. Do not say "I will send a link". |
| "Maybe, send info first" | "Will do. What is the one asset you would want first if you moved forward?" |
| "Not now" | "Can I send a short email? If things change next month, you will know where to find us." Log it. Follow up in 30 days. |
| "How much?" | "It starts at $2,500 a month. Month to month. What do you spend now across freelancers, tools and agencies?" Let them compare. |
| "No" | "Appreciate you being straight. Can I check back in 60 days?" Log it. Move on. |

### Voicemail (under 20 seconds)

> "Hey [name], [your name] from [company]. I looked at [company]'s site and had a quick thought about [specific thing]. Worth 2 minutes. Call me back, or I will try you again [day]."

### Call recovery lines

| Situation | Say |
|---|---|
| You lose your train of thought | "Sorry, lost my thread. How are you handling design workload right now?" |
| You do not know the answer | "Good question. I do not want to give you a generic answer. Can I look into it and follow up by email?" |
| You need time to think | "That is interesting. Tell me more about how your team handles that now." |
| The prospect is rude | "Sounds like this is not a pain right now. Appreciate the honesty." Then end the call. |

Universal fallback: ask any question about their team or setup.

## 6. After the call

Complete the log before the next dial.

- [ ] Company, contact, date
- [ ] Picked up, voicemail, or no answer
- [ ] Outcome: meeting booked, send info, call back, not interested, wrong person
- [ ] Current design setup
- [ ] Pain they mentioned
- [ ] Next action and date

### Follow-up emails (send within 2 hours)

Keep each one to three sentences.

- **Meeting booked:** thank them, give the booking link, link to a short "how the queue works" page.
- **They asked for info:** a short overview. Quote the thing they said on the call. Ask for 15 minutes.
- **Not now:** "No rush. Here is where to find us. I will check back in 30 days."
- **Voicemail left:** mention your observation and offer a 2-minute chat with a booking link.

### Follow-up cadence

| Day | Action |
|---|---|
| 0 | Call, voicemail and email on the same day |
| 2 | Second call at a different time |
| 4 | Short email: "Circling back on the voicemail. Worth 2 minutes?" |
| 7 | Last call. Voicemail: "Last try. Here when you need us." |
| 30 | Re-engage the "not now" group |
| 60 | Re-engage the "no" group |
| After 3 touches with no reply | Mark as cold. Look again in 90 days. |

## 7. Objection library

### Price

- **"$2,500 a month is too much."** "What do you spend now across freelancer invoices, tools and management time? Most teams are at $5,000 to $10,000 when they add it up. We are one line item that replaces all of it." Compare to their current cost. Do not defend the price.
- **"Can we do a trial or get a discount?"** "We do not do free trials. It is month to month with a 14-day money-back guarantee, so you can try it for two weeks with no risk." The guarantee is the trial. Do not discount.

### Timing

- **"Not the right time."** "When would be better? Is there a launch or campaign coming where this would matter?" Tie "later" to a real event. If you cannot, it means never.
- **"We are in the middle of a rebrand."** "When that is done you will have many new assets to ship. Can I check back so the queue is ready?" Position yourself as the execution layer after the strategy work.

### Competition

- **"We already have a designer."** "Do they cover product and GTM? We do not replace your designer. We take the GTM lane off their plate so they can focus on product." Extra points: their designer is likely spending most of the week on social graphics and decks.
- **"We tried a design subscription and it did not work."** "Fair. What broke: quality, turnaround or communication? Those are the three failure points. We built the process around them: an SLA with credits, a shared dashboard, and a founder on every project."
- **"We have a freelancer."** Freelancers are great for projects. For steady output, the burden of managing availability, revisions and invoices becomes a second job. A freelancer on holiday means your work stops. Most freelancers also do not do motion.
- **"A competitor is cheaper."** Check what you are comparing. Some services sell hours, not outputs. Some charge extra for motion. Compare finished tasks per month, not the headline price.

### AI

- **"We just use AI for design."** "It works for social and templates. It breaks where work has to convert: landing pages, ad creative, pitch decks. AI generates. We ship things that work."
- **"AI will make this obsolete."** "Maybe one day. Today teams are not stuck because they cannot generate images. They are stuck because nobody makes the calls on what converts, what fits the brand, and what is ready for production." Do not argue about the future.

### Trust

- **"You are too new."** "Fair. That is why I am calling you myself. The founder is on every project right now, and the 14-day money-back guarantee lets you see quality before you commit."
- **"Do you have case studies?"** Be honest. "Not yet. What I can offer is a 48-hour turnaround on one real asset so you can judge for yourself. That is better than a PDF." Offer proof through work, not documents.
- **"Can you match our brand guidelines?"** "Yes. At onboarding we collect your logo files, colors, fonts, tone, and examples of what to do and not do. Every task uses that brand kit. If something is off-brand, flag it in revisions and we fix it."
- **"Why do we need a tool? Slack is enough."** "Slack is great for talking. For managing design work, requests get buried, feedback sprawls, and nobody knows what is active. The dashboard has one intake form, a clear queue, SLA timers and revision history. Slack handles the conversation. The product handles the workflow."

## 8. The 15-minute walkthrough (demo) framework

Show. Do not tell. Prepare by learning their creative volume, whether they have a designer, and their biggest bottleneck. Use their company name in the demo screens.

1. **Hook (30 seconds).** Show a messy chat thread with 15 messages about one request. "This is what most teams live with. I will show you how it becomes a structured, SLA-backed workflow."
2. **Agenda and discovery (90 seconds).** Say what you will cover. Then ask how they get a design done today and how long it takes. Let them answer.
3. **Intake form (2 minutes).** Task type, deliverable, context, references, priority, preferred date. "Two minutes now saves hours of back-and-forth later."
4. **Active slots and SLA timers (3 minutes).** Show three columns: queued, active, delivered. Explain that only the active slots are being worked on, each with a countdown. Show drag-and-drop to reprioritize.
5. **Revisions (2 minutes).** Show a task with version history. A revision restarts the clock. "Unlimited revisions until it matches the brief."
6. **Motion preview (2 minutes).** Show a real motion sample. Prove motion is not an afterthought.
7. **Chat tool integration (1 minute).** Show the notifications: task active, task delivered, designer question. "Chat is for updates. The dashboard is for management."
8. **Why chat alone is not enough (1 minute).** Requests get buried, no SLA, messy feedback, no priority.
9. **Clear next step (30 seconds).** Offer two options: start on Core for a month, or go to Ops Base if you already need design and motion in parallel. Onboarding takes 48 hours: collect brand guidelines, set up the dashboard, connect chat, submit the first task.
10. **Q and A (2 minutes).** Be ready for case studies, brand fit, and pause questions.

Checklist:

- [ ] Hook with their pain in the first 30 seconds
- [ ] Show intake, slots and timers, revisions, motion and chat updates
- [ ] Answer "why not just Slack" with proof
- [ ] Give a clear next step with two plan options
- [ ] Deal with case studies and brand fit before they ask

## 9. ROI and pricing talk tracks

Cost comparison we used on calls (our estimates, not audited. Check yours before use):

| | Our Core plan | Freelancers | Agency | In-house hire |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Monthly cost | $2,500 | $5,000 to $15,000, variable | $10,000 to $25,000 retainer | $12,000 to $20,000 loaded |
| Turnaround | 48h SLA | 1 to 3 weeks | Weeks to months | Depends on the queue |
| Motion included | Yes | Often extra | Often extra | Another hire |
| Predictability | Active slots | Hope | Contract | Bandwidth |
| Exit risk | Month to month | Ghosting | Locked contract | Bad hire |

**Annual math.** "A full-time designer costs about $144,000 to $240,000 a year with salary, benefits, tools and management time. Our Core plan is $30,000 a year. Design and motion, 48-hour turnaround, pause when work slows down."

**Cost of doing nothing.** "Every week your landing page stays un-optimized and your ads stay stale, you leave pipeline on the table. The question is what those stuck assets cost you."

## 10. Call timing

Call between 9 and 11 AM in the buyer's local time. We called from India, so the best slots fell at awkward hours. Plan your own table.

| Buyer region | Their 9 to 11 AM is about this in India time | Priority |
|---|---|---|
| UK | 2:30 to 4:30 PM | High |
| Germany, Nordics | 1:30 to 3:30 PM | High |
| US East | 7:30 to 9:30 PM | Critical. Most buyers fit here. |
| US West | 10:30 PM to 12:30 AM | High if you have named leads |
| Dubai | 10:30 AM to 12:30 PM | Medium |
| Australia | 4:00 to 6:00 AM | Low |

(Times shift with daylight saving.) Look for overlap windows where one session reaches two regions. Late afternoon India time reaches the UK and Germany together.

## 11. Weekly targets and kill rules

Targets we set (goals, not measured results):

| Metric | Weekly target | Expected rate |
|---|---|---|
| Calls placed | 20 | 5 per session, 4 sessions |
| Conversations | 4 to 6 | About 25% connect rate |
| Meetings booked | 2 | About 40% of conversations |
| Proposals sent | 1 to 2 | About 50% of meetings |
| Closes | 1 every 2 weeks | About 50% of proposals |

### Checkpoints (review after every 10 calls)

| Check | If it is low | Action |
|---|---|---|
| Connect rate under 20% | Bad timing or bad numbers | Change time blocks. Verify numbers. |
| People hang up in 20 seconds | The observation is weak | Make it more specific. |
| Conversations die after the opener | The pain question is weak | Rework the transition. |
| They talk but do not book | The close is weak | Tighten the bridge to the meeting. |
| You hear the same objection again and again | A gap in your answer | Drill that response. |

### Kill thresholds

| After | If | Then |
|---|---|---|
| 10 calls | 0 connects | Change time blocks. Verify numbers. |
| 5 conversations | 0 interest in the problem | The ICP may be wrong. Review who you call. |
| 3 meetings | 0 progress to close | Fix the walkthrough. The script is fine. |
| 20 calls | 2 or more meetings | The system works. Raise the volume. |

## 12. Phrases to avoid

| Do not say | Why | Say instead |
|---|---|---|
| "We are a design subscription service" | Nobody knows what it means. Sounds like a template tool. | "We are a design pod for SaaS growth teams." |
| "Unlimited requests" | Sounds too good to be true. | "Unlimited revisions until it matches the brief." |
| "Marketing consultancy plus design" | Too many ideas. | "We help growth teams ship faster." |
| "Sorry to bother you" | You have something worth saying. | Start with the opener. |
| "Is this a good time?" | It never is. | "Got 2 minutes?" |

## Adapt it

- Pick your own ICP table before you write a single script.
- Write your own observation templates for your craft (code review, copy, analytics, whatever you sell).
- Keep the rule that the first call sells a 15-minute meeting, not the product.
- Keep the kill thresholds. They stop you from calling the wrong people for a month.
- If you cannot show case studies, say so. Then give a risk-free way to see real work.
