# Sako playbooks

These are the working documents of a closed startup, cleaned up and shared in public. They are for future founders who want to build a productized service business, especially one that sells to B2B software teams. Each playbook says what we did, gives you templates and checklists, and tells you how to adapt it. Take what helps. Ignore the rest.

Private details are removed: no client or prospect names, no contact details, no lead lists, no investor names, and no personal data.

## What Sako was

Sako was a productized design subscription for B2B SaaS teams. Clients paid one monthly fee, put requests in a queue, and got delivery in 48 hours for each active slot. We also built a go-to-market engine to sell it.

Two founders ran it.

- **Sanjay** looked after clients and design, and ran four designers.
- **Koushik** looked after product and go-to-market, ran one HR person and one growth specialist, and built all the software alone with AI agents.

| September 2025 | First revenue |

We shared everything because the hard part was distribution, not the idea or the software. We also did not love the agency model.

## A note on numbers

Our old documents did not always agree. Prices, margins and revenue figures changed between versions. Where they conflicted, these playbooks use the last price list and the headline numbers above. Targets are marked as targets. Treat anything that is not a headline number as a planning figure, not a result.

## Lessons we learned

- **Distribution was the hard part.** We could build, price and deliver. Reaching buyers was slower and harder than any of those. If you can only improve one thing, improve how you reach buyers.
- **Revenue came about nine months in.** We started at the end of 2024 and earned nothing until September 2025. Plan your runway for a long wait, not a 30-day launch.
- **Growth was real but small.** About $7,000 a month for eight months, with 10% to 40% growth. It was far below the plans we had written.
- **Our plans ran far ahead of our evidence.** The scaling models and decks described many delivery teams and very large revenue. Plan from measured numbers. Label everything else as an assumption.
- **A services business stays a people business.** Productizing made delivery predictable. It did not remove the need for designers, capacity planning and constant sales. We did not love the agency model, and the product did not change that.
- **Building the software was not the bottleneck.** One person built all of it with AI agents. That speed is a big advantage, but it does not fix distribution.
- **We sold without proof.** At the start we had no case studies, so the sales scripts leaned on founder attention, a 48-hour trial asset and a money-back guarantee. Get proof early.
- **Keep one source of truth.** We had several price lists and several revenue numbers across documents. It confused buyers, investors and us.
